# Enterprise Cybersecurity Governance Framework — AIMG

A senior-level cybersecurity governance portfolio case study for **Atlas Industrial Machinery Group (AIMG)**, a fictional multinational heavy-equipment manufacturer operating a hybrid enterprise IT and industrial OT environment.

## Project objective

The project demonstrates how cybersecurity can be governed as a business-risk discipline rather than a collection of technical controls. It connects business criticality, risk ownership, Crown Jewels, resilience, policy and control governance, metrics, assurance and continuous improvement into one management framework.

## Organization model

AIMG is modeled as a large multinational manufacturer with:

- 25,000 employees
- 12 manufacturing plants
- Approximately 20,000 IT and OT assets
- 20 Azure subscriptions
- Hybrid on-premises, cloud and industrial OT/ICS environments
- 24×7 security operations

## Governance capabilities demonstrated

### Business alignment
- Eight enterprise business processes assessed for criticality
- Financial, operational, customer, legal/regulatory, safety and reputation impact dimensions
- Accountable business owners identified for material processes
- Technology dependencies linked to business outcomes

### Crown Jewel and CIA governance
- Eight critical enterprise assets identified
- Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability requirements assessed
- Enhanced focus on identity, ERP, MES, PLM, engineering data and OT/ICS
- Business dependency used to justify protection priority

### Cybersecurity risk governance
- Inherent risk, control effectiveness and residual risk concepts
- Risk treatment, formal acceptance and delegated decision authority
- Escalation when exposure exceeds approved tolerance or authority
- Risk register, remediation and evidence expectations

### Governance operating model
- Executive and management oversight
- Business Owner and Risk Owner accountability
- Information Security / Cybersecurity oversight and challenge
- IT, OT, cloud and application control execution
- Three Lines accountability and independent Internal Audit assurance
- Governance RACI and decision authority

### Policy and control governance
- Cybersecurity document hierarchy
- Policy ownership, approval, review and exceptions
- Control ownership and effectiveness validation
- Compliance monitoring and remediation governance

### Performance and executive reporting
- KPI, KRI and KGI definitions
- RAG thresholds and management actions
- Reporting cadence and escalation
- Data-quality and validation expectations
- Management-focused cyber reporting

### Assurance and continuous improvement
- Control assurance and audit planning
- Finding severity and remediation governance
- Legal, regulatory, privacy and contractual compliance governance
- External assurance and certification governance
- Governance maturity assessment
- Improvement roadmap and annual framework review

## Project boundary

AIMG is a fictional organization created for professional portfolio and learning purposes. Organizational facts, recovery assumptions and governance decisions are modeled and do not represent a real employer or client environment. In a production implementation, business criticality, RTO/RPO values, risk appetite, delegated authorities and control requirements would require formal stakeholder validation and approval.
