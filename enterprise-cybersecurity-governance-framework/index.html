Business problem

Cybersecurity decisions need business context.

Technical findings alone do not tell management what deserves priority. This project converts business dependency, operational impact and asset criticality into a repeatable governance decision model.

01

Ownership ambiguity

Technical teams may operate controls, but business and risk accountability must remain with authorized decision-makers.

02

Competing priorities

Manufacturing, finance, supply chain, identity and engineering systems require different protection and recovery priorities.

03

Risk visibility

Material cyber exposure should not disappear inside operational queues; it needs thresholds, escalation and management reporting.

04

Assurance gap

Control implementation must be validated through evidence, independent review, audit and continuous improvement.

02 / Crown Jewels

Protect what the business cannot afford to lose.

Crown Jewels are identified using business dependency and confidentiality, integrity and availability requirements—not technology popularity.

03 / Governance operating model

Separate ownership, oversight, execution and assurance.

The framework uses clear decision authority and Three Lines accountability so cyber risk remains owned by the business while security provides challenge, guidance, monitoring and escalation.

Business accountability

Business Owner + Risk Owner

Own business outcomes, approve treatment choices within delegated authority and formally accept residual risk when authorized.

AccountableDecision authorityRisk acceptance
Security oversight

Information Security / Cybersecurity

Defines requirements, assesses exposure, recommends controls, challenges risk decisions, monitors remediation and escalates material issues.

Technical execution

IT / OT / Cloud / Application Teams

Implement and operate controls, maintain technical compliance, remediate findings and produce evidence of completion.

Independent assurance

Internal Audit / Independent Review

Provides independent validation of governance, risk management and control effectiveness without owning remediation.

Governance decision path

Every material risk gets an owner, a decision and an evidence trail.

01IdentifyBusiness process, asset, threat, vulnerability or control gap
02AssessBusiness impact, likelihood, inherent risk and control effectiveness
03DecideTreat, transfer, avoid or accept within authority
04EscalateRoute exposures above tolerance or delegated authority
05VerifyTrack evidence, residual risk and independent closure
04 / Performance & management reporting

Measure what management can act on.

Metrics are designed to support decisions, show risk movement and trigger escalation—not simply count security activity.

KPI

Performance

Are security processes and remediation activities being executed as expected?

ExamplesPatch SLA, control implementation, remediation completion
KRI

Risk exposure

Is cyber exposure moving toward or beyond the organization’s approved tolerance?

ExamplesCritical overdue risks, high-risk exceptions, unsupported systems
KGI

Governance outcome

Is cybersecurity governance delivering the intended business and risk-management result?

ExamplesRisk reduction, resilience improvement, assurance maturity
RAG

Management action

Thresholds translate metrics into Green, Amber or Red signals with defined response and escalation.

PurposeTurn reporting into decisions
05 / Assurance & continuous improvement

Governance must prove that it works.

Assurance combines control testing, audit, compliance review, evidence validation, external assessment and maturity improvement.

Control assuranceDesign + operating effectiveness

Validate that controls are appropriately designed and operate as intended.

Audit findingsRisk-based remediation

Assign owners, deadlines, severity, evidence and independent closure.

ComplianceObligations → controls

Translate regulatory, legal, privacy and contractual obligations into accountable requirements.

MaturityContinuous improvement

Use recurring assessment, roadmap actions and annual review to improve governance capability.

What this project demonstrates

  • Governance thinking: cybersecurity aligned to enterprise objectives and decision authority.
  • Risk management: business impact, prioritization, residual risk, acceptance and escalation.

Important: This is a fictional professional portfolio case study. It does not represent a real client or employer environment, and it does not claim actual organizational approval. Recovery assumptions would require formal Business Impact Analysis and owner validation in a production organization.
