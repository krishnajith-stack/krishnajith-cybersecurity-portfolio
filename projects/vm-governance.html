Enterprise Vulnerability Management Governance Model

A risk-based operating model based on enterprise vulnerability-management practices: prioritize what matters, assign ownership, govern remediation, manage exceptions and prove closure.

This page describes a generalized portfolio operating model. Metrics shown in the main portfolio are drawn from my stated enterprise experience; no confidential asset or vulnerability data is published here.

Problem

Scanner severity alone creates noise. Enterprise teams need a consistent way to combine technical severity with exploitability, exposure, asset criticality, business impact and vulnerability age.

Objective

Drive Critical and High vulnerabilities to remediation within agreed SLAs while maintaining traceability for blockers, accepted risk, exceptions and validation.

Operating Workflow

1
Discover & normalizeIdentify assets, ownership and vulnerability findings across IT and OT environments.
2
Prioritize riskCombine CVSS, exploitability, threat intelligence, exposure, asset criticality, business impact and aging.
3
Assign remediationRoute findings to infrastructure, application, endpoint, network, cloud or OT owners with clear due dates.
4
Govern exceptionsDocument blockers, compensating controls, risk owner approval and expiry dates.
5
Validate closureRescan or verify remediation evidence before closure.
6
Report & escalateTrack SLA compliance, aging, recurrence, risk acceptance and systemic remediation blockers.

Management Measures

  • Critical / High SLA compliance
  • Open findings by age and business unit
  • Recurring vulnerabilities
  • Exception volume and expiry
  • Remediation blockers
  • Risk-reduction trend

Leadership Interfaces

  • SOC and threat-intelligence teams
  • Infrastructure, application and endpoint owners
  • Network and cloud teams
  • OT engineering teams
  • Risk / compliance and business owners
  • Vendors and service providers

What this demonstrates

My approach to vulnerability management is governance-led rather than scanner-led: evidence, risk context, ownership, remediation discipline and measurable closure.
