Enterprise Cybersecurity Governance Framework — AIMG

A governance case study for a fictional industrial enterprise designed to connect business objectives, IT/OT risk, accountability, control design, management reporting and continual improvement.

ISO 27001:2022NIST CSFIT / OTRisk GovernanceRACIKRIs / KPIs
Scope note: This is a fictional portfolio case study created to demonstrate governance thinking. It does not represent confidential material from a real employer or client.

Business Context

AIMG is modeled as a global industrial organization with hybrid IT and OT environments, cloud services, critical business applications, production systems and geographically distributed operations.

Governance Objective

Translate cybersecurity from a technical activity into a business-governed capability with clear ownership, risk decisions, control accountability, escalation paths and measurable outcomes.

Framework Flow

1
Understand the businessIdentify critical services, information, production dependencies, regulatory drivers and business impact.
2
Identify cyber riskBuild scenarios around threats, vulnerabilities, exposure, business criticality and CIA impact.
3
Assign ownershipSeparate business risk ownership, control ownership, technical implementation and independent assurance.
4
Define controls and treatmentSelect preventive, detective and corrective controls aligned to risk treatment decisions.
5
Measure and reportUse KRIs, KPIs, exceptions, incidents and remediation trends to support management decisions.
6
Improve continuouslyFeed incidents, audits, risk reviews, threat intelligence and operational lessons back into the governance cycle.

Key Deliverables

  • Organization and technology landscape
  • Business-impact and CIA assessment
  • Risk scenarios and treatment logic
  • Governance roles and accountability model
  • Control and policy structure
  • Management reporting approach

Standards Alignment

  • ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — ISMS governance and risk-based controls
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework — Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover
  • ITIL concepts — operational ownership, incident/change/problem interfaces

What this demonstrates

The project shows how I would move from business context to risk ownership, security control governance and executive reporting rather than treating cybersecurity as a collection of disconnected tools. It complements my operational background in vulnerability management, infrastructure and security operations.
